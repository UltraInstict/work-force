Z83 Form Explained: How to Apply for Government Jobs in South Africa

Applying for a public service job is different from applying in the private sector. Almost every national and provincial department asks for the same form, the Z83, and a mistake on it can get you screened out before anyone reads your CV.

What is the Z83?

It's the standard Application for Employment form used across the public service. Because every applicant fills in the same form, departments can compare candidates fairly.

What goes on it

  • Personal details: full name as on your ID, date of birth, ID number, contact details
  • Post details: the job title, reference number and department from the advert
  • Qualifications: highest qualification, institution, year obtained
  • Work experience: employer, position, dates and main duties, most recent first
  • References: people who can speak to your work

Mistakes that cost applicants

  1. Leaving sections blank. Write "N/A" if something doesn't apply.
  2. Wrong or missing post reference number.
  3. Contact details with typos.
  4. Illegible handwriting. Type it if you can.
  5. Sending a general CV instead of one that matches the post.

Where to find vacancies

Departments advertise in the Public Service Vacancy Circular and on their own sites. You can also browse government jobs in South Africa by province in one place.

Strengthen your CV first

Your Z83 is only half the application. Check sample CVs for layout ideas, or build your CV online.

Closing tip: Read the advert's closing date and submission instructions twice. Rules differ between departments, and a late or wrongly submitted application is simply discarded.
